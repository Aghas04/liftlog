# Where the form tips come from

The How to sheet (js/howto.js) and the key cues (js/tips.js) mix two kinds of advice:

- **Studied**: tested in research. These are shown in the sheet under "What studies show", with the source.
- **Coaching**: standard technique taught by coaches (for example "elbows pinned on curls" or "don't swing"). It is sensible and widely agreed, but most of it has never been tested in a trial cue by cue. The sheet says so.

Audit, November 2026: every claim was checked. The ones below had research behind them, or were changed because the research said something different.

## Changed after checking

| Before | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy lifts: "hold your breath through the hard part" | Breathe out through the hardest part. A brief brace of one breath is OK on heavy sets (about 5 reps or fewer). Never hold your breath with high blood pressure or a heart condition | The NSCA advises breathing out through the sticking point. Holding the breath (Valsalva) stiffens the trunk but sharply raises blood pressure (Hackett & Chow 2013; MacDougall 1985: up to 480/350 mmHg on the leg press) |
| Leg press: no breathing note | Breathe out on each push; never hold your breath for several reps | MacDougall 1985: the highest blood pressures of any lift measured |
| Incline bench "about 30°" | "about 30-45°" | Rodríguez-Ridao 2020: the upper chest worked most at 30°, with 45° close behind; steeper shifts work to the front delts |
| Squat: "most quad and glute growth comes from the bottom half" | Deep squats grew the glutes and inner thighs more; quads grew about the same | Kubo 2019: glutes +6.7% vs +2.2%, adductors +6.2% vs +2.7%, quads similar |
| Leg curl: "point the toes up" | Removed. Now: full range, and seated curls grow the hamstrings more | No evidence for the toe cue. Maeo 2021: seated beat lying, +14% vs +9% |
| Calves: "slow, full reps grow calves" | Spend time in the deep stretch at the bottom | Rep speed from 0.5 to 8 s grows muscle about equally (Schoenfeld 2015). The stretched range matters more (Kassiano 2023) |
| Abs and raises: "slower reps / lighter weight work it more" | "Controlled reps" / "a weight you can control" | Same: speed itself isn't the factor (Schoenfeld 2015); control and effort are |
| Upright row "lift to chest height" | Elbows no higher than the shoulders, wide grip | Schoenfeld, Kolber & Haimes 2011 |
| Dead hang "great for shoulder health" | Removed. Now: "builds grip strength" | No good evidence for the shoulder claim |
| Russian twist: no back note | People with sensitive backs can use a cable rotation or anti-rotation hold instead | Loaded spinal rotation while bent forward is debated (McGill). Kept as a cautious option, not a claim |

## Checked and kept

| Cue | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Bench: elbows about 45°, not flared to 90° | Expert opinion on shoulder stress (Green & Comfort 2007). Not tested in trials (Noteboom 2024) | Expert opinion; the sheet says so |
| Squat: knees may travel past the toes | Fry, Smith & Schilling 2003: stopping the knees raised hip and lower-back torque | Moderate |
| Pulldown to the front, not behind the neck | Signorile 2002: front wide grip gave as much or more lat activity; later EMG studies found little difference | Moderate |
| Overhead triceps work for the long head | Maeo 2023: overhead grew the triceps more than the arms-down position (long head +28.5% vs +19.6%) | Moderate |
| Seated calf raises work the soleus | With the knee bent, the gastrocnemius is slack (anatomy) | Strong |
| Lateral and front raises: thumbs level or up, slightly in front of the body | Thigpen 2006: thumbs-down ("empty can") tips the shoulder blade and narrows the tendon space, and is more painful for people with impingement. One imaging study found no difference in the gap itself | Moderate |
| Lunges: a longer step is easier on the knees; leaning forward works the glutes more | Escamilla 2008 (long vs short step, kneecap stress); Farrokhi 2008 (trunk lean) | Moderate |
| Leg press: feet higher for glutes, lower for quads | A higher foot position means more hip bend and less knee bend (geometry); not checked against a growth study | Coaching |
| Timed holds: breathe steadily | Avoids the blood-pressure spike of breath holding | Moderate |
